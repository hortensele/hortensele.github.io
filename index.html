Web app · Lung adenocarcinoma

LungScope

Predicts overall survival from routine H&E lung adenocarcinoma slides. Point it at a folder of slides and it tiles each one, groups tiles into Histomorphological Phenotype Clusters (HPCs) (recurring tissue patterns found by self-supervised learning), then scores risk from each slide's HPC composition.

  • Run: submits a 7-step GPU pipeline to the cluster under the user's own account, with live progress.
  • Results: Cox risk scores and tertiles, plus concordance index and Kaplan–Meier curves against a clinical file.
  • Slide explorer & HP-Atlas: synced H&E / HPC views and a browsable atlas of every HPC.

Web app · Expert validation

TissueScope

A blinded tissue-morphology rating tool. Pathologists see 28 grids of tile crops, each from one automatically derived cluster, with no labels or metadata. For each grid they rate on a 1–5 scale whether the tiles belong together and give a short free-text guess at the tissue type.

  • Purpose: tests whether unsupervised clusters correspond to real, recognizable morphology.
  • Designed for busy experts: about 30–45 minutes total, with progress saved so raters can stop and resume.

Open-source code · Colorectal cancer

PanColon-CHiPS

The released pipeline behind my colorectal cancer survival work: an interpretable disease-free survival model on H&E slides from 1,024 patients across 16 institutions, validated on held-out external centers (C-index 0.68).

  • Benchmarks self-supervised phenotype clustering against pathology foundation models (UNI, CONCH, TITAN, H-optimus).
  • Every risk score traces back to the tissue morphologies driving it.
  • Two repos: the paper code, and an inference pipeline so others can validate the model on their own external dataset.
